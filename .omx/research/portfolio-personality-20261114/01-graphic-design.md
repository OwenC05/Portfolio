## What is observed, and what is inferred

I inspected the current concept HTML, brand specification, desktop and mobile captures. **Observed:** oversized Bodoni identity, beige surfaces, graphite research section, orange accents, an isolated generated switch illustration, thin rules, diagrams and a late hobbies sentence. Audio is absent from that sentence. The brand specification correctly identifies the illustration as generated rather than Owen's hardware.

**Interpretation:** the draft feels basic because its attractive ingredients do not form a sufficiently personal visual argument. The name and image occupy adjacent rectangles but have little compositional relationship. The switch supplies tactile detail that the rest of the page does not share. Most sections return to large type, spacious columns and fine rules, so changes of background provide more variation than the underlying visual language. Bodoni's extreme stroke contrast suggests a fashion or cultural editorial identity, but that association has not been connected to Owen's interests. On mobile, the individual illustration becomes another large block after the work index. The portfolio names hobbies without letting their particular character shape its world.

This is a design judgement. Serif type, restraint and generated imagery need not look artificial; specificity comes from a particular person's choices and activity.

## Five primary references and transferable lessons

1. **[Mason Wong, About](https://masontywong.com/about).** Observed live: warm architectural surfaces, a circular illuminated portrait frame, recognisable person and straightforward biography/navigation. Light gives otherwise simple geometry depth; portrait scale supplies a clear focal point. Borrow the connection between atmosphere and a human presence, not the peach room or circular portrait treatment.
2. **[Sharlee](https://itssharl.ee/), including [Work](https://itssharl.ee/work).** Observed live: the menu reuses the homepage's iridescent curved forms and distinctive wide type; hovering the Dua Lipa row changes the adjacent visual preview. The identity persists across states. Borrow this repeatable vocabulary and close connection between navigation and content, not its pastel blobs or letterforms.
3. **[Jesse's Ramen](https://www.jesse-zhou.com/).** Observed in the supplied scene capture: a compact shop silhouette, concentrated neon lighting, bright physical signposts and dark surrounding space. The lesson is selective density: a detailed focal world inside a quiet frame. Inference: signs help make the scene interpretable. I did not independently test navigation or performance.
4. **[Teenage Engineering, EP–133](https://teenage.engineering/products/ep-133).** Observed live: oversized outlined product typography over boxing photography; the official page also presents sequencing, component details and tactile controls. A product metaphor connects campaign expression to practical explanation. Borrow the alternation of expressive scale and precise detail, not a replica sampler interface or unsupported claims about Owen's equipment.
5. **[Bruce Mau Design, Sonos](https://www.brucemaudesign.com/work/sonos).** The studio describes an identity designed for variation and a shift toward the music experience; its displayed applications combine photography, repeated typography and graphic patterns. Borrow consistent rules that permit different compositions. A collection of identical cards is not the only way to achieve coherence.

## Three genuinely different directions

### 1. Signal Bench — graphic, tactile, primarily two-dimensional

**First screen:** a strong, compact Owen Cheung masthead above an asymmetric working surface. One large project artifact occupies the centre/right; a narrow left column states the role and links directly to selected work. A small personal object crop overlaps the artifact's margin, establishing scale and authorship without obscuring content.

**Type:** a sturdy, slightly condensed or square grotesk for identity; an open, readable sans for prose. Restrict technical-looking numerals to meaningful labels. Test the actual name and project titles before choosing a font.

**Palette/materials:** cool light grey and deep ink, electric blue for selection, a small warm orange secondary accent. Think matte panel plus one translucent or brushed material; shallow consistent shadows, not metallic gradients everywhere. Squared panels and occasional circular marks repeat only where their roles make sense.

**Density:** medium, with one dense evidence cluster and generous surrounding space. Project labels, image captions and diagrams share alignment and spacing rules.

**Why Owen:** music production, listening equipment, keyboards and engineering suggest tools and feedback. This is a proposed connection, not a claim about his practice. Coffee and snowboarding can appear in personal photographs without becoming fake controls.

**Tradeoff:** strongest fit for legible engineering evidence and feasible without a room, but risks looking like another hardware brand. Actual artifacts and a distinctive name treatment must prevent that. Avoid decorative meters, fake operating statuses and controls that do nothing.

### 2. After-hours Studio — a staged spatial portrait

**First screen:** one tightly cropped studio corner fills roughly two-thirds of the view; readable name, role and project links occupy a calm strip beside it. A working screen or printed artifact is the brightest useful surface. A headphone silhouette and an object associated with making music can coexist, provided Owen confirms them. A snowboard edge could create a vertical counterweight; do not force all four hobbies into the hero.

**Type:** confident humanist sans, with compact lettering for captions. The name remains clear outside the scene rather than becoming tiny text on a prop.

**Palette/materials:** ink blue, warm white light and one muted cyan accent. Limit dominant materials to dark matte surfaces and warm wood, with a small glass or metal detail. One motivated light source, coherent shadows and separated foreground/background values create depth. Rounded equipment shapes contrast with the rectilinear architecture.

**Density:** concentrated in the scene, low elsewhere. Case studies move to readable flat layouts that retain its colours and caption system.

**Why Owen:** offers warmth and a sense of shared space while accommodating his interests. It most directly explores what he may like about Mason and Jesse.

**Tradeoff:** highest asset burden and greatest risk of a generic rendered desk. A photographed or illustrated fixed scene could establish the direction; an explorable full room is an additional proposal requiring a reason. No device brands, possessions or room details should be invented as biography.

### 3. Field Journal — colourful print composition, no room

**First screen:** a bold typographic title, one large cropped photograph or project image, and three numbered work entries arranged like an edited magazine spread. One deliberate diagonal crop introduces movement while body text stays level. Personal and technical material share the same caption system.

**Type:** condensed, robust display lettering paired with a neutral reading face; emphasis comes from scale and width rather than many fonts.

**Palette/materials:** paper white, black, cobalt and acid yellow, with a restricted two-colour treatment for supporting imagery. Matte print texture belongs to images or a small graphic area, not an overlay on every paragraph. No simulated room lighting; hard graphic edges and contrasting image crops carry depth.

**Density:** alternating energetic spreads and quiet reading sections. Large numerals and controlled repetition establish rhythm.

**Why Owen:** holds snowboarding imagery, keyboard details, listening, making music and coffee without putting everything in one physical space or reducing interests to icons.

**Tradeoff:** depends on strong personal material; without it, the result could resemble an invented lifestyle brand. Its relationship to the already-liked working table is less direct.

## Opposing view, questions and handoff

My ranking favours engineering clarity. If Owen values a personal world more strongly, After-hours Studio should move first. Sparse editorial work could also succeed with distinctive photography and typography; busier is not automatically better.

Questions for the coordinator to carry into discussion: Which feeling matters most—precise/playful, intimate/atmospheric, or energetic/personal? Which real objects, photographs or music-making artifacts would Owen want represented? Should visitors first meet the person or inspect a project? Equipment, genres, DAW, experience, locations and routines remain unknown.

Next decision: choose a visual premise and authentic source material before commissioning a visual. Later evaluate whether a still desktop/mobile view communicates identity, role and a project destination; whether materials, colours and shapes have clear purposes; and whether project qualifications remain visible.
