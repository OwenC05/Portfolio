## Three organising metaphors, ranked

### 1. The working table: things currently being made

**Proposal:** a deliberately composed surface with project evidence in the foreground and two or three personal traces around it. A genuine keyboard detail, a small musical draft and a photograph could share one material and lighting language; they need not all appear together. Snowboarding can enter through a personal note rather than an implausibly positioned board.

The organising question is “What are you working on or enjoying here?” Literal project labels and optional artifact inspection could work in a shallow illustration before any 3D decision.

**Why first:** it preserves the concept Owen already likes and can connect physical making with software. **Tradeoff:** easily becomes an aspirational desk advertisement. Avoid generic premium gear, invented handwriting and simulated clutter with no story.

### 2. Field notebook: things noticed, tried and kept

**Proposal:** a small edited collection of observations, experiments and moments. Project studies provide the main structure; occasional dated photographs, listening notes, a musical sketch or a keyboard comparison supply another register. Use real dates only when known. Visual cues could be contact sheets, precise captions and contrasting image scales, without fake paper aging.

The organising question is “What caught your attention, and what happened next?” Optional interactions expand a photograph or compare two genuine iterations.

**Why second:** accommodates indoor and outdoor interests equally and tolerates incomplete work honestly. **Tradeoff:** a sparse archive can look abandoned or assign Owen an unwanted blogging obligation. Start with a finite selection, not a promised weekly journal.

### 3. A personal programme: selected work, listening and interludes

**Proposal:** an intentionally sequenced programme, borrowing pacing from a listening session or small music publication. Software studies are clearly named main items; a short personal interlude offers an original musical fragment, listening recommendation, photograph or coffee note. Confident typography and pauses establish rhythm without drawing a literal mixing desk.

The organising question is “What would you choose to share with someone spending a few minutes here?” Listening and making get distinct labels. Playback is optional and never unlocks navigation.

**Why third:** potentially distinctive if music is central to Owen's identity. **Tradeoff:** currently unproven; album-style presentation could falsely imply a professional recording practice. Promote this only after understanding his relationship with music. Avoid invented release histories and fake audio meters.

## Hobby-to-content map

These are possible story prompts, not claims about Owen. Do not allocate one object to every row.

| Interest | Honest theme to explore | Visual cue and meaningful content | Optional interaction; restraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listening / headphones / hi-fi | Attention, taste, changing preferences | One authentic setup detail or listening note explaining what he enjoys hearing | Link to a chosen recording or reveal the note; omit gear prestige and unsupported sound-quality measurements |
| Music production | Play, revision, rhythm, learning | An original short fragment with one sentence about a choice or unfinished question; a real session crop only if shareable | Play/stop, or compare genuine versions; no autoplay, invented DAW or implied release credentials |
| Snowboarding | Movement, practice, place, time away | A personal image or recollection, with location only if confirmed; its composition can inform spatial contrast | Expand the image or show a real sequence; avoid synthetic trip memories, ability claims and decorative piste maps |
| Coffee | Ritual, sensory preference, a pause | One actual routine or preference, perhaps a photograph; precision only if Owen actually enjoys that aspect | Reveal a short note; omit grind charts and brew dashboards unless they describe his real practice |
| Custom keyboards | Touch, sound, choice, modification | An actual build detail and why he chose or changed it | Optional owned sound recording or before/after comparison; omit a shopping catalogue and invented components |

**Connection to work:** let visitors notice shared patterns such as revision or tuning; do not assert causation. A keyboard story may sit near TypeForge, but it does not prove learning gains or explain the product's origin without confirmation. Project claims retain their own evidence: exploratory dissertation benchmark, Application Copilot in development, and TypeForge's actual product/model work. Personal experimentation cannot qualify those results.

## Two proposed first visits

**The hiring reader:** sees Owen's name, software/applied-AI focus and direct project choices immediately. Opens TypeForge or the dissertation, understands Owen's contribution and limitations, then notices a small personal detail. Leaves with one credible work example and one human memory. Exploring scenery is optional.

**The curious peer:** notices a personal caption, opens an original audio fragment or photograph, and reads why Owen chose it. A nearby project link returns to substantive work. Sound stays off until requested; the same meaning is available in text.

## Opposing view, missing inputs and handoff

**Blind spot:** this lens may overvalue authenticity through artifacts. Owen may prefer a stylised invented world or privacy over personal photography. That can still work when presented as illustration; coherent art direction may matter more to him than documentary specificity. The supplied references establish attraction, not the reason for it.

The coordinator should resolve three inputs before art direction:

- Which interest feels most like Owen, and is audio primarily listening, making, or equally both?
- What is one specific, true story or preference he would enjoy explaining? Which details should stay private?
- What existing material may be shown: one personal image, one original sound/sketch, or one real build/process detail? A missing artifact is a reason to simplify, not invent.

**Handoff:** discuss the working table first, field notebook second, programme third; select one organising rule before scenery, objects or animation. Ask Owen to react to these narratives, then pass the chosen direction and authentic inputs to the coordinator's design synthesis. Do not generate or implement yet.
