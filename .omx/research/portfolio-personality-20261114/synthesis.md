## Three discussion directions

### 1. After-hours Studio — atmospheric and spatial

An angled view into a small, inhabited studio corner: dark matte surfaces, a warm desk light, ink-blue surroundings and a restrained coloured accent. One useful surface, such as a project screen or open experiment notebook, supplies the focal point. Clear name, role and direct work links remain readable outside the scene.

Listening and music-making can both have a presence. Keyboards connect naturally to TypeForge. Coffee can provide a personal ritual, and snowboarding can appear through a print, photograph or other owner-chosen detail. Do not require every hobby in the first frame. Props are proposals; none establish what Owen owns.

Interaction draws the visitor closer to a meaningful artifact. Project views retain the scene's type, colour and caption language while giving technical material enough reading space. The ambition is a recognisable place with a useful interface, not a generic premium desk advertisement.

### 2. Signal Bench — graphic, tactile and instrument-inspired

A more compact, precise working surface influenced by the relationships between tools, feedback and making. Strong sans-serif identity, cool grey and deep ink, selective electric blue/orange, deliberate crops and shallow material depth. One project artifact leads the composition; personal details interrupt it at an intentional scale.

This could be highly expressive without a complete room. Its danger is becoming another hardware-brand imitation or ornamental dashboard. Actual evidence, distinctive composition and controls with real purposes must carry the personality.

### 3. Field Journal — energetic and personal

Bold typography, photography, contact-sheet-like selections and edited project spreads. Paper white/black with cobalt and acid yellow is one proposed palette, not a chosen brand. Snowboarding and music can shape pacing and imagery without needing to belong to a desk scene.

This is the strongest non-spatial alternative. It needs specific material to avoid resembling an invented lifestyle publication, and should be a finite curated collection rather than an unwanted blogging commitment.

Do not impose one equally weighted object per hobby. Interests may express pleasure, rest or taste without proving professional competence. Do not invent possessions, releases, trips, handwriting, recipes or experience to make a scene look lived-in.

## Sound and motion proposal

Sound is optional, explicitly enabled, immediately stoppable and independent of motion settings. Listening and making should remain distinct stories. A short owner-approved musical fragment could be more personal than generic interface bleeps. Keyboard/interface cues, if used, should be sparse and tied to deliberate actions; ordinary scrolling, focus and typing need not make noise.

The sound researcher proposes pressure and placement as the motion vocabulary: a key depresses, a print comes forward, a selected artifact settles. Reduced-motion presentation uses immediate state changes and stationary content. These are creative policies, not claims about tested browser behaviour or mandatory legal thresholds. Playback, interruption and volume behaviour need later device testing.

Do not use decorative meters, fake live agent execution, or an animated successful recovery as evidence that those systems work. Original or appropriately licensed audio needs provenance; no audio has been generated, played or selected yet.

## Keeping the engineering work substantial

The dissertation needs a readable paired example and retrieved context, plus configuration/provenance and qualifications. Application Copilot needs a genuine recorded workflow or explicitly labelled synthetic test trace. TypeForge should eventually offer an actual product interaction rather than only a decorative case-toggle. LNRS should describe employer-safe developer-workflow contributions honestly.

Project labels and direct destinations stay easy to find and share. Spatial exploration can enrich the visit without being required to locate work/contact. Personal details and technical evidence should reinforce recall while keeping their claims separate.
