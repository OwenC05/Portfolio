## Why area is insufficient

A strip **0.85 m × 0.0015 m** has area **0.001275 m² = 12.75 cm²**. Splitting that rectangle diagonally produces two **6.375 cm²** triangles. Both exceed N1's **4 cm²** threshold, even though the strip can be an intentional bevel surface with smoothly varying corner normals. For example, a normal turn distributed around a 30° bevel segment can give corner normals roughly 15° from the segment's geometric normal, appropriately failing a 2° flat-face test applied to the wrong class.

Conversely, subdividing a domed-shading panel into smaller triangles could remove it from an area-selected test without improving its normals. Triangle area, aspect ratio and geometric coplanarity alone do not distinguish a broad panel from a flat tessellation facet on a curved bevel. Every ordinary triangle is itself planar. Neither exported normal direction nor its agreement with the face may determine the class being tested: that would make the test circular.

## Recommended exact verification contract

1. **Author-declared surface groups.** Before normal generation, actual Claude declares source face groups within each affected casing part: `planar_panel` versus `bevel_or_round`, plus explicitly named remaining surfaces outside N1's casing scope. The declaration identifies leaf, part, stable source-face/group identity and intended class. Broad shell top/front/side panels and bevel bands must have distinct identities even when merged into one ledger leaf. Claude owns that classification and the chosen shading treatment; Codex validates its application. A new version must not silently reclassify a failing panel as bevel.
2. **Complete export correspondence.** Record a separate, hash-bound technical sidecar mapping each exported triangle `(leaf, primitive, triangle ordinal)` to its declared source group and class. Preserve the source polygon/corner correspondence or verify it against the evaluated geometry during export; do not assume Blender/exporter triangle or vertex ordering survives. Require every in-scope triangle to map exactly once, no overlaps or missing entries, and reconcile per-group source/export triangle counts and geometric coverage. Bind the sidecar to exact source/helper and GLB hashes, record expected group coverage, and reject ambiguous mappings. Where the exporter triangulates polygons differently, establish correspondence from the evaluated export topology or verified polygon coverage, rather than guessed triangle indices. This is offline evidence, **not** a new public glTF attribute/extras field or additional material/leaf.
3. **Flat-panel assertion.** For **every** exported triangle classified `planar_panel`, with actual accessor positions `p0,p1,p2`, form `g = normalize((p1-p0) × (p2-p0))`. For each of its three referenced exported `NORMAL` values `ni`, require finite, valid unit normals under the unchanged profile and `dot(normalize(ni), g) >= cos(2°) = 0.9993908270190958`. Do not use `abs(dot)`, average the three errors, recompute replacement normals, or discard failures. Degenerate triangles cannot establish a face direction and must be reported/rejected rather than silently omitted. Apply the assertion regardless of area; retain area only as a diagnostic if useful.
4. **Bevel/round assertion boundary.** Do not require these normals to equal individual facet normals. They remain subject to the unchanged finite/unit-normal/profile checks and any separately authored normal-field or seam-continuity specification. In particular, a smooth panel–bevel boundary may share an endpoint normal equal to the panel normal while the turn occurs farther across the bevel. No arbitrary bevel angle threshold or requirement for a hard normal seam is introduced here. Independent images still decide whether the surfaces read correctly.
5. **Receipt.** Report each group's class, source/export counts, unmapped/ambiguous count (required zero), planar maximum corner error and every failing triangle/corner locator. Include planar/bevel triangle counts and areas so coverage changes are visible. Hash-binding plus explicit group coverage prevents an all-bevel relabelling from masquerading as a passing flat-panel audit.

Suggested replacement for N1's sentence: **“On every exported triangle belonging to an author-declared planar casing-panel group, each referenced exported vertex normal must be within 2° of that triangle's oriented geometric normal. Bevel/round groups are explicitly identified and excluded from this flat-panel assertion; complete, hash-bound source-to-export group coverage is required. Triangle area does not select the groups.”**

## Existing pipeline and coordinate caveats

The frozen helper's `Part` stores only vertices plus `(face indices, UV rule)` at `build_fullkit_m4_geometry.py:93–115`; it contains no persistent shading-purpose classification. Box/extrusion primitives at `:120–141` already construct explicit face lists, making primitive-authored group identities a concrete place for the author to add such evidence. Do not pretend the current data already distinguishes panels and bevel bands.

The frozen builder at `build_fullkit_m4.py:110–135` recalculates face normals, marks faces smooth and marks edges sharp only above 40°. It does not write custom split normals there. Export at `:355–360` applies evaluated data and converts Z-up to glTF Y-up. Its separate inherited `join` routine at `:321–339` reconstructs vertices/polygons/UVs and recomputes smoothing without copying custom corner normals; any future route through such reconstruction would need to preserve/reapply the intended normals. This is a pipeline consideration, not a claim that unstable v03 currently uses that path for new M4 casings. Source custom-normal assignment alone is insufficient evidence; the test must inspect the final exported NORMAL accessor.

Define the 2° check in **final glTF mesh-local space**, using positions and normals from the same primitive and indexed corner. Export vertex splits at UV/normal seams are allowed and must be followed through indices. Do not compare Blender Z-up face vectors to glTF Y-up normals. If a world-space check is separately required, transform positions with the full matrix and normals with its inverse transpose, renormalize, and handle reflected winding explicitly; nonuniform scale can change the angle, so state which space owns the tolerance. The runtime's uniform ×2 scale and translation preserve angles but multiply areas by four—another reason not to use an unstated area space as the selector.

Custom per-corner normals add no triangles by themselves but can split exported vertices and affect geometry bytes. Preserve all row/profile/retained-data limits and remeasure actual compressed output as already required. Generic face weighting is an authoring method, not proof of the 2° outcome; the exported geometric test is decisive. Root/Claude should settle the narrow wording and exact group coverage before it becomes an acceptance gate. This focused review is complete.
